Will this booking cancel?

Hotel Aurelia's revenue desk sees every new reservation arrive with two facts attached: how many days ahead it was booked, and the nightly rate. History shows which of those bookings were later cancelled and which guests stayed. k-nearest neighbors predicts a new booking's fate by asking: what happened to the k most similar bookings we've already seen? The desk then decides whether to resell (overbook) the room.

Why small k makes islands

  

    

    
At k = 1, every booking claims its own territory

    
The prediction anywhere on the chart is simply the outcome of the single closest past booking. The chart splits into one region per booking (Voronoi cells). A kept booking sitting among cancellations gets its own gold region, and the boundary has to loop around it. That loop is an island.

    
Many oddball bookings are just noise

    
Each past outcome was drawn at random from its true cancellation probability. A booking with an 85% chance of cancelling still had a 15% chance of being kept. That gold dot in the maroon zone is luck, not a rule. At k = 1 the model memorizes every lucky outcome, which is why training accuracy is 100% while held-out accuracy drops.

    
Larger k turns each prediction into a local average

    
At k = 15 the oddball is one vote among fifteen and gets outvoted, so its island disappears. An island only survives if a whole cluster of same-outcome bookings sits together, which is far more likely to be a real pattern.

    
Overlapping neighborhoods make the boundary smooth

    
Nudge a new booking slightly and at k = 1 its one neighbor can switch, flipping the prediction from 0% to 100%. At k = 15 a small move swaps maybe one neighbor in fifteen, shifting the vote by about 7 points. Predictions change gradually, so the boundary becomes a smooth curve.

    
This is the bias–variance trade-off

    
As a rule of thumb, k-NN behaves as if it has about n ÷ k effective parameters. Small k: low bias, high variance; the boundary would look completely different with a new history. Large k: more bias, low variance.

    
Too large a k erases real patterns too

    
Early-bird promo bookings (far ahead, low rate) genuinely rarely cancel. A moderate k keeps that pocket as a legitimate island. At k = 41 it gets outvoted by its high-risk surroundings. At k = 140, every prediction is the overall cancellation rate and there is no boundary at all.
